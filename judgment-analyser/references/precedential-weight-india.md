# Precedential Weight — Indian Law

Load this during Step 5.

---

## Article 141

The law declared by the Supreme Court is binding on all courts within the territory of
India. Three points a senior reads into that:

1. **"Law declared"** means the ratio, not everything in the judgment.
2. **"All courts"** does not include the Supreme Court itself — it is not bound by its
   own decisions, though it follows them absent good reason.
3. **A dismissal without reasons declares no law.** An SLP dismissed in limine, or by a
   non-speaking order, is not a declaration under Article 141 and does not merge the
   judgment below into it.

Article 144 separately requires all authorities to act in aid of the Supreme Court.

## Bench Strength

The governing rule: a bench is bound by decisions of benches of equal or greater
strength, and cannot overrule a coordinate bench.

| Situation | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Larger bench vs smaller bench | Larger prevails |
| Coordinate bench disagrees with earlier coordinate bench | Must refer to a larger bench; cannot simply differ |
| Two conflicting coordinate-bench decisions exist | The position is unsettled. Flag FRAGILE. Practice varies — some courts follow the earlier, some the later, some the better reasoned |
| Later smaller bench purports to differ from a larger bench | The later decision is vulnerable; the larger bench decision holds |
| Constitution Bench (5+) | Overrides all smaller benches on the point |

Record the coram count explicitly in the case note. "Two-judge bench" and
"Constitution Bench" are not interchangeable authorities, and a case note that omits
bench strength has withheld the most important fact about the judgment's weight.

## Binding Scope by Forum

- **Supreme Court** → binds every court in India.
- **High Court, Division or Full Bench** → binds subordinate courts within that state
  and single judges of the same High Court. Persuasive only in other states.
- **High Court, Single Judge** → binds subordinate courts within that state. A
  coordinate single judge who disagrees must refer to a larger bench.
- **Between High Courts** → persuasive only. There is no vertical relationship. Note
  the state expressly whenever citing a High Court judgment.
- **Tribunals** → an appellate tribunal binds the tribunals below it. First-instance
  tribunal orders are not precedent.
- **Foreign judgments** → persuasive only, and their weight depends on how closely the
  statutory framework matches. Say what the differences are rather than citing them
  flatly.

## Doctrines That Undercut Weight

**Per incuriam.** A decision rendered in ignorance of a binding statute or a binding
precedent is per incuriam and does not bind. The bar is high: it is not enough that
the earlier authority was not cited; it must be one that would have changed the
outcome. Where a per incuriam argument is genuinely available, set out the specific
provision or authority overlooked and the paragraph where the court proceeded without
it. Where it is merely arguable, say so and do not overstate.

**Sub silentio.** A point that was assumed rather than argued and decided is not
binding on that point. Look for propositions the court applied without discussion —
these are the sub silentio candidates.

**Obiter relied on as ratio.** The most common weakness in a citation. Cross-check the
Step 4 table before assessing weight.

**Distinguishable on material facts.** Not a defect in the judgment, but a limit on its
reach. List the material facts that must be matched for the ratio to apply.

**Pending reference.** If the judgment records that a question has been referred to a
larger bench, or if a conflicting line exists, the position is not settled.

## Subsequent History — the Mandatory Caveat

**This skill cannot verify whether a judgment is still good law.** It reads the text in
front of it. Whether the judgment has since been overruled, doubted, distinguished,
referred to a larger bench, or superseded by amendment cannot be determined from the
four corners of the document.

Every weight assessment must therefore close with an express statement to that effect,
directing the reader to check current status on SCC OnLine, Manupatra, or the court's
own website before citing. This is not boilerplate — a case note that implies
currency it has not verified is worse than no case note.

Also check for statutory supersession: a judgment interpreting a provision that has
since been amended or replaced may be entirely correct and entirely useless. Note the
provision and its version where visible from the text.

## The Three-Band Flag

- **STRONG** — larger or adequate bench, squarely on the point, ratio (not obiter),
  no conflict or per incuriam concern visible on the face of the record.
- **QUALIFIED** — binding or persuasive but limited. Distinguishable facts, a narrow
  issue, split or concurring reasoning, coordinate-bench conflict, or the useful
  proposition sits in obiter.
- **FRAGILE** — per incuriam risk, sub silentio, obiter being pressed as ratio,
  interim, consent or Article 142 order, dismissal without reasons, or a pending
  reference to a larger bench.

State the band, then state the reason in one sentence. A band without a reason is a
guess with a label on it.
