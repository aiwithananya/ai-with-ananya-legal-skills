# Anatomy of an Indian Judgment

Load this during Step 1. It tells you where to find each element and what not to trust.

---

## Standard Structure

Indian judgments do not follow a mandatory format, but most contain these components
in roughly this order:

1. **Cause title** — court, bench, case number and type, parties, date.
2. **Coram** — the judges. Count them. Bench strength drives precedential weight.
3. **Appearances** — counsel for each side. Useful for spotting concessions later.
4. **Introduction / nature of the proceeding** — what is being challenged and under
   what provision. This tells you the standard of review.
5. **Facts** — often long, often including procedural history through the courts below.
6. **Questions for determination** — sometimes numbered, sometimes buried in prose.
7. **Submissions** — appellant first, then respondent, usually attributed to counsel
   by name ("Mr. X, learned Senior Counsel, submitted...").
8. **Analysis and reasoning** — the operative part.
9. **Conclusion and directions** — the disposition, costs, and any operative orders.
10. **Separate opinions** — concurring or dissenting, if any.

Interim orders, consent orders and short dismissals may contain almost none of this.
Say so rather than forcing them into the template.

## Paragraph Numbering

Cite paragraph numbers, never page numbers — pagination differs across SCC, AIR, SCC
OnLine, and the court's own PDF, while paragraph numbers are stable.

Where a judgment is unnumbered (common in older High Court decisions and in some
tribunal orders), say so explicitly and cite by section heading plus a short quoted
phrase so the reader can locate the passage.

Where a judgment has separate opinions, paragraph numbering may restart. Cite as
"¶12 (per Nagarathna J.)" to avoid ambiguity.

---

## Headnotes — Do Not Rely on Them

Headnotes in SCC, AIR and other reporters are written by editors, not by the court.
They are summaries, sometimes wrong, sometimes reflecting obiter as though it were
holding. They are not part of the judgment and are not binding.

**Rule for this skill:** if the available text is a headnote, digest, editorial note,
or news report, stop and say so. Do not produce a case note from it. The one
permitted use of a headnote is as a locator — to find the paragraphs worth reading in
the full text.

---

## Citation Conventions

Order of preference when citing:

1. **Neutral citation** where available — e.g. `2024 INSC 123`. The Supreme Court and
   most High Courts now issue these; they are court-issued and reporter-independent.
2. **SCC** for the Supreme Court — `(2023) 4 SCC 456`.
3. **AIR** — `AIR 2023 SC 1234`.
4. **SCC OnLine** for unreported or recently reported matters — `2024 SCC OnLine SC
   789`, `2024 SCC OnLine MP 456`.

Give the case name in full on first use, then a short form. For High Court judgments,
always name the High Court — "binding" means nothing without knowing which state.

Where a citation cannot be confirmed from the document itself, record it as [BLANK]
with a reason rather than reconstructing it. A wrong citation in a case note travels
into pleadings.

## Court Hierarchy and What It Means for Reading

| Forum | Note when reading |
|---|---|
| Supreme Court | Article 141 — declares law binding on all courts in India |
| High Court (Division Bench) | Binding on subordinate courts and single judges of the same High Court; persuasive elsewhere |
| High Court (Single Judge) | Binding on subordinate courts in that state; a coordinate single judge who disagrees must refer, not decide |
| High Court (Full Bench) | Binding within the state; resolves intra-court conflict |
| NCLAT / appellate tribunals | Binding on the tribunals below them; subject to appeal |
| NCLT / trial tribunals | Not precedent; persuasive at best |
| Arbitral award | Not precedent at all. Binds only the parties |

For writ jurisdiction, note whether the court was exercising Article 226 (High Court,
wider) or Article 32 (Supreme Court, fundamental rights). The scope of the remedy
constrains what could be decided.

## Signals Worth Recording

- **"Per incuriam"** used by the court about an earlier decision — record it, it is
  doing real work.
- **"Referred to a larger bench"** — the judgment may not be the last word. Flag as
  FRAGILE.
- **"In the peculiar facts of this case"** or **"under Article 142"** — the court is
  signalling that the order is not to be treated as precedent. Article 142 orders in
  particular do complete justice between the parties and are routinely stated not to
  be precedent.
- **"We are not inclined to interfere"** in an SLP dismissal — dismissal of an SLP
  without reasons is not a declaration of law and does not merge the judgment below.
  Say so if the document is such a dismissal.
- **Guidelines issued pending legislation** — operative, widely followed, but note
  they are directions rather than statutory interpretation.
