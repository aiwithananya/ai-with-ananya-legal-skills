# Statutory Framework: Section 138 NI Act

Quote operative words from the bare Act. Do not paraphrase from memory. Where a rule below depends on case law, say so and ask the advocate to confirm current authority instead of citing a case from memory.

## The offence (S.138)
A cheque drawn on an account maintained by the drawer, for the discharge of a legally enforceable debt or other liability, returned unpaid by the bank for insufficiency of funds or because it exceeds the arrangement. Courts have read other return reasons (for example "account closed", "payment stopped by drawer") into the section in many situations. Flag the return reason for the advocate to confirm.

Punishment: imprisonment up to two years, or fine up to twice the cheque amount, or both.

## The three provisos (all must be satisfied)
(a) Cheque presented within six months from its date, or within its validity period, whichever is earlier. Bank validity is currently three months. Check against the date on the cheque.
(b) Payee issues a written demand notice within 30 days of receiving information from the bank about the dishonour.
(c) Drawer fails to pay within 15 days of receiving the notice. The cause of action arises on the expiry of these 15 days.

## Limitation for the complaint (S.142(1)(b))
Complaint must be filed within one month of the date the cause of action arises under proviso (c). The court may take cognizance after this period if the complainant shows sufficient cause for the delay. If late, draft a separate application for condonation and flag it.

## Computing the timeline
- Exclude the first day (day of receipt) when counting the 30-day and 15-day periods.
- "One month" is a calendar month.
- Where the notice was returned "refused", "unclaimed" or "left", service may be presumed if it was correctly addressed and sent by post. Flag this for the advocate; it depends on the facts.
- Show every date as a calendar date in a table: memo received, notice sent, notice served, 15 days expire, cause of action, last date to file, planned filing date.

## Jurisdiction (S.142(2))
- If the cheque was delivered for collection through an account: the court where the branch of the payee's bank (where the payee maintains the account) is located.
- If presented otherwise than through an account: the court where the drawee bank branch (where the drawer maintains the account) is located.
Plead the facts that give the court jurisdiction in a separate paragraph.

## Companies and firms (S.141)
Every person who was in charge of and responsible for the conduct of the business at the time of the offence is liable, along with the company. Plead specifically, for each director or partner, what role they had. Bare repetition of the words of the section is risky. Non-executive and independent directors need specific averments.

## Other provisions to know
- S.139: presumption that the cheque was for a debt or liability.
- S.143: summary trial.
- S.143A: interim compensation up to 20% of the cheque amount. Offer to draft the application.
- S.145: evidence of the complainant may be given on affidavit.
- S.147: offence is compoundable.
- S.148: appellate court may order deposit of at least 20% of the fine or compensation.

## Procedure under the BNSS, 2023
The complaint is filed before the Magistrate and the complainant is examined under Section 223 of the BNSS. The proviso to Section 223 requires that the accused be given an opportunity of being heard before cognizance is taken. Ask the advocate to confirm how the court applies this to S.138 complaints. Also ask whether the court has any current practice directions for S.138 complaints (for example a synopsis or a fixed format on the first page).
