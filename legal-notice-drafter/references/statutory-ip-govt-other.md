## Type 11 — Intellectual property cease and desist

### Read this before drafting anything

Indian IP statutes create a cause of action **against the sender** of an unjustified
threat. This is the single most important thing about this notice type.

- Trade Marks Act, 1999, s.142 — groundless threats of legal proceedings
- Copyright Act, 1957, s.60 — groundless threats
- Patents Act, 1970, s.106 — groundless threats
- Designs Act, 2000, s.23 — applies s.106 of the Patents Act

The person threatened may sue for a declaration that the threat is unjustifiable, an
injunction against continuance of the threats, and damages. The usual protection is
that the sender institutes and pursues with due diligence a suit for infringement.

**Consequence for drafting:** Establish the client's registered right, or the facts
supporting passing off, **before** the notice goes out, and confirm the client is
prepared to sue. Ask the user directly: "Is the client willing to file if this is
ignored?" If the answer is no, say that a bare cease and desist carries s.142
exposure and offer a without-prejudice commercial letter instead.

### Substantive basis

- **Registered trade mark:** s.29, Trade Marks Act, 1999. Plead the registration
  number, class, date of registration and the goods or services covered.
- **Unregistered mark:** passing off, preserved by s.27(2). Plead goodwill,
  misrepresentation and damage. Registration particulars are not available, so the
  factual case must be built out.
- **Copyright:** s.51, Copyright Act, 1957. Registration is not a precondition to
  subsistence; plead authorship, originality and the chain of title.
- **Patent:** s.48, Patents Act, 1970. Identify the patent number and the claims said
  to be infringed. Do not assert infringement without a claim mapping.

**Period to give:** 7 to 15 days. Shorter where continuing infringement is causing
ongoing damage and an interim injunction is contemplated.

**Mandatory contents:**
- Particulars of the client's right, with registration details where registered
- Identification of the infringing use, with dates, URLs, listing numbers or
  photographs, and how it was discovered
- The specific acts to cease
- Ancillary demands: withdrawal of listings, delivery up, disclosure of stock and
  suppliers, undertaking not to repeat
- Reservation of the right to sue, without a threat that outruns the client's
  instructions

## Type 12 — Notice before suit against Government or a public officer

**Provision:** Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, s.80(1).

**Mandatory:** Yes, for a suit against the Government, or against a public officer in
respect of an act purporting to be done by that officer in official capacity.

**Period to give:** Two months. The suit cannot be instituted until two months have
expired after delivery of the notice.

**Mandatory contents — s.80(1) is specific:**
- The name, description and place of residence of the plaintiff
- The cause of action
- The relief claimed

**Delivery:** To, or left at the office of, the officer specified in s.80(1) for the
Government concerned — the Secretary to Government, or in the case of a suit against
the Central Government relating to the railways, the General Manager, and so on.
Identify the correct officer for the specific defendant before drafting.

**Urgency:** s.80(2) permits a suit with the leave of the court without serving
notice where urgent or immediate relief is sought, but the court must not grant
relief without giving the Government or officer a reasonable opportunity to show
cause.

**Defects:** s.80(3) — a suit is not to be dismissed merely by reason of an error or
defect in the notice, if the notice conveys the name, description and residence of
the plaintiff, and identifies the cause of action and the relief claimed. This is a
saving provision, not a licence to draft loosely.

## Type 13 — Consumer grievance notice

**Provision:** Consumer Protection Act, 2019.

**Mandatory:** No. A complaint may be filed without a prior notice. The notice is
useful for establishing the deficiency and the opportunity to cure, and it often
resolves the matter.

**Limitation:** Two years from the date on which the cause of action arose (s.69),
with power to condone delay on sufficient cause shown.

**Pecuniary jurisdiction of the follow-on complaint** — as amended with effect from
2021, computed on the value of the goods or services **paid as consideration**:
- District Commission — up to ₹50 lakh
- State Commission — above ₹50 lakh up to ₹2 crore
- National Commission — above ₹2 crore

`[VERIFY — these limits have been revised more than once; confirm before advising on
forum.]`

**Threshold check:** Is the client a "consumer" under s.2(7)? Goods or services
obtained for a commercial purpose are excluded, subject to the self-employment
exception. A business-to-business supply dispute is generally not a consumer
complaint — redirect to type 2.

**Mandatory contents:** The transaction with dates and amounts, the defect in goods or
deficiency in service, the loss suffered, the relief sought, and the period to cure.

## Type 14 — Defamation

**Civil:** No statutory notice requirement. Limitation is one year — Limitation Act,
1963, Art. 75 for compensation for libel, running from publication, and Art. 76 for
slander.

**Criminal:** Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, s.356, which replaced ss.499 and 500 of
the Indian Penal Code with effect from 1 July 2024. A complaint would lie under BNSS
s.223. **Do not cite IPC ss.499/500 in a notice issued today.**

**Advise before drafting.** A defamation notice is frequently counter-productive:
- It can itself be published, amplifying the original statement
- Truth, fair comment and the exceptions to s.356 are available defences, and a
  notice invites the recipient to assemble them
- Where the statement concerns a matter of public interest, the notice may attract
  criticism as an attempt to suppress speech

Put this to the client and record that it was put. Then draft if instructed.

**Mandatory contents:** The exact words complained of, quoted verbatim, with the date,
place and medium of publication; the imputation said to arise; the reference to the
client; the damage; and the relief — takedown, apology in specified terms and
placement, and damages.

## Type 15 — General purpose notice

For a demand that fits no statutory profile.

**Period to give:** 15 days unless the user specifies otherwise.

**Structure:** Follow `references/drafting-conventions.md` in full, omitting the
statutory basis paragraph and substituting the contractual or common law basis of
the claim.

**Mandatory:** State expressly in the covering message to the user that no statutory
profile was applied, and identify what would need to be verified before issue.

## Limitation Act, 1963 — working reference

| Claim | Article | Period | Runs from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price of goods sold and delivered, no credit period | 14 | 3 years | Date of delivery |
| Price of goods, credit period agreed | 15 | 3 years | Expiry of credit period |
| Money payable for work done | 18 | 3 years | When the work is done |
| Money lent | 19 | 3 years | When the loan is made |
| Compensation for breach of contract | 55 | 3 years | When the contract is broken |
| Libel | 75 | 1 year | Date of publication |
| Slander | 76 | 1 year | When the words are spoken |
| Possession based on title | 65 | 12 years | When possession becomes adverse |
| Suit for which no period is provided | 113 | 3 years | When the right to sue accrues |

**s.18** — a written, signed acknowledgement of liability made before expiry starts a
fresh period from the date of acknowledgement. Always ask whether one exists.

**s.19** — part payment of a debt, where the fact of payment appears in the
handwriting of the payer, likewise starts a fresh period.
