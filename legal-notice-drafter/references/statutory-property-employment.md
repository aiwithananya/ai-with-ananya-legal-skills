## Type 7 — Termination of lease / notice to quit

**Provision:** Transfer of Property Act, 1882, s.106, as amended by Act 3 of 2003.

**Threshold question, ask it first:** Does a State rent control statute govern these
premises? If it does, that statute displaces s.106 and eviction lies only on the
statutory grounds it specifies. Go to type 8. Do not draft a s.106 notice for
premises covered by rent control and assume it will support a suit for possession.

**Periods (in the absence of a contract or local usage to the contrary):**
- Lease for agricultural or manufacturing purposes — deemed year to year,
  terminable by **six months'** notice
- Any other lease — deemed month to month, terminable by **fifteen days'** notice

**Post-2003 position:** The notice need no longer expire with the end of a month of
the tenancy. A notice is not invalid merely because the period stated falls short of
the statutory period, provided the suit or proceeding is instituted after the
statutory period has expired.
`[VERIFY — confirm the current text of the proviso to s.106 before relying on the
short-period saving.]`

**Form and service:** Must be in writing, signed by or on behalf of the person giving
it, and served under s.106(4) — tendered or delivered personally to the party or one
of his family or servants at his residence, or sent by post to the party, or, if that
cannot be done, affixed to a conspicuous part of the property.

**Mandatory contents:**
- Identification of the premises with sufficient particularity
- The lease or tenancy relied on, with its date and how it arose
- The ground of termination, or an express statement that the tenancy is being
  determined by efflux of the notice period
- The date on which the tenant is required to deliver vacant possession
- A demand for arrears and mesne profits, if claimed, stated separately from the
  demand for possession

**Do not conflate** determination of the tenancy with a demand for arrears. They are
different claims with different consequences, and a composite demand that reads as
an offer to continue the tenancy on payment can waive the determination.

## Type 8 — Eviction under State rent control legislation

**Provision:** Varies by State. This skill does not carry the grounds for every
State Act. Identify the governing statute, then state to the user that the grounds
and notice requirements must be confirmed against it.

Statutes likely relevant to this practice:
- Madhya Pradesh Accommodation Control Act, 1961 (Bhopal and MP generally)
- Karnataka Rent Act, 1999 (Bengaluru)
- Delhi Rent Control Act, 1958

**Mandatory approach:** Draft the notice around the specific statutory ground relied
on — bona fide requirement, default in payment, subletting, material alteration,
change of user — and plead the ingredients of that ground. A rent control eviction
notice that recites only s.106 language is of little use.

Mark the entire statutory basis `[REQUIRES VERIFICATION]` and tell the user which
provision needs checking. Do not reconstruct State rent control provisions from
memory.

## Type 9 — Employment: termination or show cause

**Threshold question:** Is the recipient a "workman" within s.2(s) of the Industrial
Disputes Act, 1947? The answer changes the entire analysis. It turns on the nature of
the duties actually performed, not the designation on the letterhead.

**If a workman, and the termination is retrenchment:**
- s.25F — one month's written notice stating reasons, or wages in lieu, plus
  retrenchment compensation of fifteen days' average pay for every completed year of
  continuous service, plus notice to the appropriate Government
- Available only after one year of continuous service as defined in s.25B
- Non-compliance renders the retrenchment void

**If not a workman:** The contract governs, read with the Shops and Establishments
Act of the State in which the establishment is registered. Notice periods under
those Acts vary; identify the State and mark the provision `[REQUIRES VERIFICATION]`.

**Show cause notice — natural justice requirements:**
- State the specific allegations with dates and particulars, not conclusions
- Identify the rule, policy or contractual term said to have been breached
- Supply or offer inspection of the documents relied on
- Give a reasonable time to reply, stated as a calendar date
- Do not pre-judge — a show cause notice that announces the finding is worthless

**Never draft** a show cause notice and a termination letter in the same run. If the
user asks for both, produce only the notice and say why.

## Type 10 — Employee-side claim for dues or wrongful termination

**Provision:** The employment contract, read with whichever of these applies:
- Payment of Wages Act, 1936 — for wages within the Act's coverage
- Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972 — s.4, gratuity on five years' continuous service at
  fifteen days' wages per completed year; s.7 requires the employer to determine and
  pay within thirty days of it becoming payable, with interest on delay
- Payment of Bonus Act, 1965
- Employees' Provident Funds and Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952
- State Shops and Establishments Act

**Period to give:** 15 days is customary.

**Mandatory contents:**
- Dates of joining and cessation, and the designation held
- Each head of the claim computed separately — salary in lieu of notice, unpaid
  salary, gratuity, leave encashment, reimbursements, bonus, incentive
- The provision or contractual clause under which each head is claimed
- Where a document is withheld — relieving letter, Form 16, experience certificate —
  demand it expressly

**Caution on restrictive covenants:** If the employer has invoked a post-termination
non-compete, note that s.27 of the Contract Act renders agreements in restraint of
trade void, subject to the sale-of-goodwill exception, and that Indian courts have
generally declined to enforce post-employment non-competes. Do not overstate this in
the notice; state the position and reserve the argument.
