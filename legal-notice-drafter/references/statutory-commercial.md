# Statutory Profiles — Commercial Notices

Load only the section for the identified type.

## Type 1 — Cheque dishonour demand notice

**Provision:** Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, s.138, proviso (b).

**Mandatory:** Yes. Absolute condition precedent. No valid notice, no prosecution.

**Outer limit to issue:** 30 days from receipt of information from the bank that
the cheque has been returned unpaid. The trigger is receipt of the return memo, not
the date on the memo. Establish the actual date of receipt and record how it was
established.

**Period to give:** 15 days from receipt of the notice by the drawer.

**Follow-on:** Complaint under s.142(1)(b) within one month of the date on which the
cause of action arose, i.e. the day after the 15-day period expires. Filed before a
Magistrate; procedure under BNSS ss.223–226 (formerly CrPC ss.200–203).

**Mandatory contents:**
- Cheque number, date, amount, drawer bank and branch, drawee bank and branch
- Date of presentation and date of return, with the reason stated in the memo
- That the cheque was issued in discharge of a legally enforceable debt or liability,
  and the particulars of that debt
- An express demand for payment **of the cheque amount**
- The 15-day period

**Consequence of defect:**
- Omitting the demand for the cheque amount is fatal.
- Demanding more than the cheque amount is not fatal where the cheque amount is
  separately and clearly identifiable within the total demanded.
  `[VERIFY CITATION — Suman Sethi v. Ajay K. Churiwal, (2000) 2 SCC 380]`
- Notice sent to a stale or wrong address, without more, risks the whole
  prosecution. Use every address on record, including the one on the cheque.

**Company drawers:** Make the company the principal noticee and name the directors
or signatories sought to be proceeded against under s.141 in their own right,
describing the capacity in which each was in charge of and responsible for the
conduct of the business.

**Jurisdiction note for the follow-on:** s.142(2) — the court within whose
territorial jurisdiction the branch of the payee's bank where the cheque was
delivered for collection is situated.

## Type 2 — Breach of contract / recovery demand

**Provision:** Indian Contract Act, 1872 (ss.73–74 for damages). No statutory notice
requirement. The obligation, if any, comes from the contract.

**Mandatory:** No — unless the contract has a notice-and-cure clause, in which case
that clause is binding and its period and mode of service must be followed exactly.
**Always ask for the contract and read the notice clause before drafting.**

**Period to give:** As the contract specifies. Where silent, 15 days is customary.

**Limitation (Limitation Act, 1963):**
- Art. 55 — compensation for breach of contract — 3 years from when the contract is
  broken
- Art. 14 — price of goods sold and delivered, no fixed credit period — 3 years from
  date of delivery
- Art. 15 — where a credit period was agreed — 3 years from expiry of that period
- s.18 — a written acknowledgement of liability signed before expiry starts a fresh
  period. Ask whether one exists; it often does, in an email or a ledger confirmation.

**Interest:** Contractual rate if provided. Absent a rate, the Interest Act, 1978
governs pre-suit interest, and s.34 CPC governs pendente lite and future interest.
Do not assert a rate without a basis.

**Cross-reference:** s.12A Commercial Courts Act — see the index file.

**Liquidated damages caution:** Where the claim rests on a liquidated damages clause,
s.74 of the Contract Act caps recovery at reasonable compensation not exceeding the
stipulated sum, and proof of loss is generally still required. Do not draft the
notice as if the stipulated figure were automatically payable.

## Type 3 — Operational creditor demand notice

**Provision:** Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, s.8, read with Rule 5 of the
Insolvency and Bankruptcy (Application to Adjudicating Authority) Rules, 2016.

**Form:** Form 3 (demand notice) or Form 4 (copy of the invoice attached to a
notice). Use the prescribed form. A free-form letter is not a s.8 notice.

**Threshold:** Default of ₹1 crore or more. Raised from ₹1 lakh by MCA notification
dated 24 March 2020. `[VERIFY — confirm the threshold is unchanged before relying.]`

**Period to give:** 10 days.

**Service:** At the registered office of the corporate debtor. Also serve by
electronic means where an address is on record.

**Follow-on:** Application under s.9 to the National Company Law Tribunal.

**The pre-existing dispute problem — flag this to the client every time.** If the
corporate debtor replies within 10 days raising a plausible dispute that existed
before the notice, the s.9 application will be rejected. The test is whether the
dispute is genuine and not spurious, hypothetical or illusory.
`[VERIFY CITATION — Mobilox Innovations (P) Ltd. v. Kirusa Software (P) Ltd.,
(2018) 1 SCC 353]`

Before drafting, ask whether the debtor has ever disputed the invoices, raised
quality complaints, or sent a counter-claim. If so, say plainly that s.8 is the
wrong instrument and recommend type 2 or type 4 instead. **The IBC is not a
recovery mechanism, and using it as one draws costs.**

## Type 4 — Invocation of arbitration

**Provision:** Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, s.21.

**Effect:** Unless the parties have agreed otherwise, arbitral proceedings commence
on the date the request to refer the dispute to arbitration is **received** by the
respondent. Receipt, not dispatch. This date stops limitation running on the claim
(s.43 read with the Limitation Act, 1963). Record how receipt will be proved.

**Mandatory contents:**
- Identification of the contract and the specific arbitration clause, quoted verbatim
- A description of the disputes referred — describe them broadly enough to cover the
  whole claim, because disputes not raised may fall outside the reference
- An express request that the disputes be referred to arbitration
- Compliance with any appointment procedure the clause prescribes, including
  nomination of an arbitrator where the clause requires the claimant to nominate

**Defect consequence:** A defective invocation — wrong clause, no express request,
failure to follow the agreed appointment mechanism — can defeat a subsequent s.11
application. Read the clause word by word and follow it exactly.

**Multi-tier clauses:** Where the clause requires negotiation, mediation or a
reference to a senior executive before arbitration, those steps must be exhausted or
expressly waived. State in the notice which pre-arbitral steps have been complied
with and when.

**Period to give:** As the clause provides; otherwise 30 days to concur in the
appointment, after which a s.11 application to the High Court (or Supreme Court in
an international commercial arbitration) becomes available.

## Type 5 — MSME delayed payment notice

**Provision:** Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development Act, 2006, ss.15–18.

**Substance:**
- s.15 — the buyer must pay on or before the agreed date; where there is no
  agreement, within 15 days of acceptance or deemed acceptance. An agreed period
  cannot exceed 45 days from acceptance.
- s.16 — on default, compound interest with monthly rests at three times the bank
  rate notified by the Reserve Bank, from the appointed day
- s.18 — reference to the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council, which
  conducts conciliation and, failing that, arbitration

**Mandatory:** No. A notice is not a statutory precondition to a s.18 reference, but
it establishes the demand and frequently produces payment.

**Threshold check before drafting — ask both:**
1. Is the supplier registered as a micro or small enterprise (Udyam)? Medium
   enterprises do not get the s.15/16 benefit for supplies.
2. Did the registration precede the supply contract? Registration obtained after the
   transaction has been held not to attract the benefit for that transaction.
   `[VERIFY CITATION — Silpi Industries v. Kerala SRTC, (2021) SCC OnLine SC 439]`

If either answer is unfavourable, say so and redirect to type 2.

## Type 6 — Secured creditor demand notice

**Provision:** Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement
of Security Interest Act, 2002, s.13(2).

**Who may issue:** Only a secured creditor as defined in the Act, and only where the
account has been classified a non-performing asset in accordance with RBI directions.
Confirm both before drafting.

**Period to give:** 60 days.

**Mandatory contents:**
- The amount due, with the break-up
- Details of the secured assets intended to be enforced
- That the account has been classified as an NPA, and the date of classification
- A statement that failure to pay within 60 days will attract the measures in s.13(4)

**Borrower's right of representation:** s.13(3A) — the borrower may make a
representation or raise an objection. The secured creditor must consider it and, if
not acceptable, communicate reasons within 15 days. Omitting this response is a
common and fatal procedural lapse. Calendar it in the service memo.

**Follow-on:** Measures under s.13(4); borrower's remedy is an application under
s.17 to the Debts Recovery Tribunal.
