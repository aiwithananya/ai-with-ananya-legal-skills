# Service and Proof of Dispatch

A notice that cannot be proved served is worth nothing. In a s.138 matter it sinks
the prosecution. Treat service as part of the drafting job, not an afterthought for
the clerk.

---

## Modes, ranked

**1. Registered Post with Acknowledgement Due (RPAD)** — the default. Produces a
postal receipt at dispatch and a signed AD card on delivery. Send to every address on
record for the noticee, including the address printed on the cheque, the address in
the contract, and the registered office on the MCA portal for a company.

**2. Speed Post** — acceptable, and the India Post tracking record with delivery
status is admissible. Useful as a parallel channel to RPAD.

**3. Email** — send in parallel where an address is on record or has been used for
correspondence in the transaction. Retain the sent item with headers. Do not rely on
email alone unless the contract's notice clause designates it.

**4. Courier** — weaker than post because the presumption under the General Clauses
Act does not attach. Use only in addition to post.

**5. WhatsApp** — supplementary only. Retain a screenshot showing delivery. Do not
present it as the primary mode.

Where the contract contains a notice clause specifying mode and address, that clause
governs and must be complied with exactly, whatever else is also done.

---

## Presumption of service

Section 27 of the General Clauses Act, 1897 — where a document is required to be
served by post, service is deemed effected by properly addressing, prepaying and
posting it by registered post, unless the contrary is proved.

The presumption of regularity in the common course of business, formerly illustration
(f) to s.114 of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, is now carried in the corresponding
provision of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023.
`[VERIFY the section number of the BSA provision before citing it.]`

**Refusal and non-collection.** For s.138 notices, a notice returned "refused",
"unclaimed" or "not claimed" from a correct address is generally treated as duly
served, and the drawer cannot take advantage of his own avoidance.
`[VERIFY CITATION — C.C. Alavi Haji v. Palapetty Muhammed, (2007) 6 SCC 555]`

Where the envelope is returned, **do not open it**. Preserve it sealed with the
postal endorsement intact. It is the evidence.

## The dispatch file

Assemble and keep, for every notice:

- Signed office copy of the notice as issued
- Postal receipts for every address and every mode
- Tracking printouts, taken at dispatch and again on delivery
- AD cards on return
- Returned envelopes, unopened, with endorsements
- Sent email with full headers, and delivery or read receipt if available
- A dated file note recording who dispatched, when, and to which addresses

## Content of the service memo produced at Step 8

1. **Date table from Step 5**, every date absolute.
2. **Addresses used**, with the source of each.
3. **Modes used**, matching the header block of the notice itself.
4. **The date service is presumed effected**, and the assumption behind it.
5. **The date the compliance period expires**, computed from presumed service.
6. **The follow-on deadline** — when the complaint, application, plaint or reference
   must be filed, and before which forum.
7. **Any intermediate step the statute requires** — for example the fifteen-day
   response to a s.13(3A) representation under SARFAESI, which is a common and fatal
   omission.
8. **Whether pre-institution mediation under s.12A of the Commercial Courts Act,
   2015 will apply** to the follow-on suit.
9. **What to do if the notice is returned undelivered** — for this notice type,
   specifically.

Close the memo with the diary dates the office should calendar, listed as dates.
